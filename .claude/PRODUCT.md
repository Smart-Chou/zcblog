# Product

## Register

brand

> Hybrid in practice: brand-first for content surfaces (articles, archives, tags, talks), product-grade UX for tool-like surfaces (bangumi tracking, search, albums, comments).

## Users

General tech readers — developers, learners, and curious technologists browsing for tutorials, insights, and personal perspectives on programming. No single demographic; the audience spans languages and experience levels. They come to read, discover, or reference content, not to manage data or complete workflows. The blog serves as both a public resource and a personal archive.

## Product Purpose

Marx's Blog exists to share a developer's journey — tutorials, musings, tool discoveries, and hard-won lessons — in a space that feels personal rather than platform-generic. Success means readers stay to read (low bounce, high time-on-page), find related content (tags, search, archives), and recognize the voice as distinctly human rather than corporate or algorithmic.

## Brand Personality

**Warm · Editorial · Playful**

- **Warm:** The space feels lived-in, not sterile. Paper-like surfaces, vermillion accents, comfortable reading rhythms. Like a well-kept personal library.
- **Editorial:** Typography leads. DM Serif headings, considered type scale, print-inspired layout. Content is treated with care — not dumped into a generic template.
- **Playful:** Emojis in navigation, a grounded motto ("这里没有答案，但有同路人。"), anime tracking, and a willingness to be a "fellow traveler" rather than an expert. The personality is approachable, not performatively polished.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate SaaS blogs** — generic blue/white palettes, stock photography, marketing CTAs, sanitized tone
- **Medium/Dev.to clones** — interchangeable reading experiences, platform chrome dominating content, zero personality
- **Overdesigned portfolio sites** — flashy scroll animations, excessive motion, form over function, the "look what I can do" aesthetic

The blog should feel like a personal space, not a growth-marketing funnel or a design portfolio flex.

## Design Principles

1. **Content owns the surface.** Every design decision starts from the reading experience. Chrome, navigation, and decoration serve the words, not the other way around.
2. **Personality over polish.** Warmth and playfulness win over sterile perfection. The voice is human — emojis, casual asides, a willingness to be imperfect.
3. **Editorial craft, not template fill.** Typography, spacing, and rhythm are considered per-surface. The blog has a visual system, not a single card-grid template repeated everywhere.
4. **Tools earn their space.** App-like surfaces (bangumi, search, albums) get real UX attention — they're not afterthoughts bolted onto a blog layout.
5. **Accessible by default.** WCAG AA is the floor, not the aspiration. Dark mode, reduced motion, keyboard navigation, and screen-reader support are built in, not retrofitted.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target:** WCAG 2.2 AA compliance
- **Existing support:** Dark/light mode, responsive design (mobile–desktop), print stylesheet, `prefers-reduced-motion` awareness
- **To verify:** Color contrast across all surfaces (particularly muted text on tinted backgrounds), keyboard navigation for search and comment interactions, screen-reader labeling for icon-only controls
